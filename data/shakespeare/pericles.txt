Introduction to the play

Pericles
 tells of a prince who risks his life to win a princess, but discovers that she is in an incestuous relationship with her father and flees to safety. He marries another princess, but she dies giving birth to their daughter. The adventures continue from one disaster to another until the grown-up daughter pulls her father out of despair and the play moves toward a gloriously happy ending.

Early printed texts

Although not included in the 1623 First Folio, 
Pericles 
was a very popular play in print. It was first published in 1609 as a quarto (Q1) and then republished again in 1609 (Q2), then in 1611 (Q3), in 1619 (Q4), in 1630 (Q5, in two different states), and in 1635 (Q6). The first folio collection that included the play was the Third Folio (1663–64), which notoriously added seven plays to the 36 in the First Folio; of that group, only 
Pericles
 has come to be accepted as Shakespearean. (It, along with 
Two

 Noble Kinsmen
, are the only plays not in the First Folio that are widely accepted today as being of substantial Shakespearean authorship.)
